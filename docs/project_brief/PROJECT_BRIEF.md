# Project Brief

## Proposed title
A Demand-Driven Framework for India's AI Infrastructure Planning: Linking Compute, Energy, Physical Resources, and Semiconductor Dependencies

## Background
AI capability depends on more than algorithms and model architecture. It requires accelerators, memory, networking, data centres, reliable electricity, cooling, water, land, manufacturing supply chains, connectivity, financing, skills and governance. These layers interact. A shortage or delay in one layer can limit the value of investment in another.

## Problem statement — draft for validation
India's AI infrastructure planning spans multiple interdependent domains, but the available evidence is reported using different definitions, time periods and levels of certainty. GPU access, installed hardware, data-centre capacity, electricity availability and domestic semiconductor capability are not interchangeable indicators. A transparent method is needed to assess these dependencies together, identify evidence-backed bottlenecks, and prioritise interventions without assuming that complete domestic self-sufficiency is always feasible or optimal.

This is a draft, not a proven claim. Antigravity must validate and narrow it after the literature review.

## Main research question
How can India assess and prioritise the infrastructure dependencies that shape its long-term AI capability, and what requirements emerge when different levels of AI compute demand are propagated through the underlying infrastructure and semiconductor supply chain?

## Supporting questions
1. What do existing studies and official reports establish about compute, energy, data centres, water, semiconductor supply chains, connectivity and governance?
2. Which indicators are genuinely comparable across India, the United States and China?
3. What infrastructure requirements follow from hypothetical GPU deployment scenarios?
4. Which results are most sensitive to uncertain technical and planning assumptions?
5. Which interventions are best classified as domestic build, partnership-led development, or managed external dependency?
6. How can the proposed framework be updated as evidence and infrastructure conditions change?

## Objectives
- Build a source-traceable literature matrix and quantitative evidence register.
- Establish a dated baseline for India and carefully selected US/China comparisons.
- Define a five-pillar framework with indicators, dependencies, decision rules and outputs.
- Implement a reproducible demand-cascade model for 200k, 500k and 1M GPU scenarios.
- Test assumptions through sensitivity analysis.
- Derive a policy roadmap from evidence and model outputs.
- Document limitations, data gaps and validation requirements.

## Intended contribution
A conceptual planning framework, operationalised through an evidence register, a dependency map, a quantitative demand-cascade demonstration, and a transparent intervention-prioritisation method.

## Scope boundaries
- The paper is not a general history of AI, a complete geopolitical survey, a defence technology survey, or a semiconductor fabrication engineering design.
- Transformer history should be brief context only.
- Defence/public-sector AI can appear as a high-consequence use case, not a separate main topic.
- Country comparison is evidence to inform India planning, not a simplistic league table.
- Do not promise that the framework is validated merely because it is diagrammed or implemented once.
