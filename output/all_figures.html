Sovereign by Design: An Integrated Five-Pillar Framework for India's Strategic Compute and Artificial Intelligence Infrastructure

Parikshit Singh Bais, Adarsh Singh
dept. of Computer Science and Engineering
Amity School of Engineering and Technology
Amity University Madhya Pradesh, Gwalior, India
e-mail: {parikshit.bais, adarsh.singh}@amity.edu

Amity University Madhya Pradesh, Gwalior, India
e-mail: {sourav, anamidhi.shrivastava}@amity.edu
Abstract—National artificial intelligence sovereignty requires co-optimizing physical, microelectronic, and institutional infrastructure. Prevailing policy treats compute, power grids, data centres, connectivity, and governance in isolation, creating capital misallocations and grid vulnerabilities. This paper introduces the India AI Infrastructure Readiness Framework (IAIRF 2.0) and an auditable six-layer Demand-Cascade Model (DCM) parameterised using verified 2026 regulatory telemetry and foundry audits. Propagating national deployment targets bottom-up, our empirical analysis reveals four core findings. First, deploying 500,000 accelerators demands 891.8 MW of peak facility power, absorbing over half of India's operational colocation capacity. Second, the assumption that domestic sub-5 nm wafer fabrication is the binding constraint is refuted: required logic wafer starts consume merely 1.60% of global foundry capacity, whereas CoWoS advanced packaging consumes 7.41%—a 4.6-fold higher constraint intensity. Third, direct-to-chip liquid cooling slashes evaporative water withdrawal by 94.6% (saving 11 billion litres annually) and reduces facility power by 172 MW in tropical climates. Fourth, Mumbai's 52.4% capacity concentration and 20–40% dynamic AI load swings threaten transmission stability. We present a sequenced three-tier roadmap prioritising transmission corridors, packaging partnerships, and regional dispersion over premature leading-edge foundries.
Index Terms—Advanced packaging, Artificial intelligence, Compute sovereignty, Data centre cooling, High-bandwidth memory, Power grid infrastructure.
